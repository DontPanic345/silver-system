Gas, under pressure

A room with a ten-atmosphere bottle of air sealed behind one small hole, and a slab of CO2 released near the ceiling. Watch two things: the bottle bleeds down until its air is at the room's pressure, and the CO2 falls, spreads, and settles into a flat layer on the floor. No rule anywhere names CO2 or says heavy gases sink — it sinks because a gram of it takes less room than a gram of air, by the same density rule that drops sand through water. Brighter gas is denser gas.
