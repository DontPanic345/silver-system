Gas, mixing

A room with a ten-atmosphere bottle of air sealed behind one small hole, and a slab of CO2 (drawn amber) released near the ceiling. Watch the bottle bleed down until the whole room is at one pressure, and the CO2 fall — it is heavier than air, by the same density rule that drops sand through water — and then not lie on the floor as a layer. Gases share cells here, so it mixes up into the air above it and the room ends up with more CO2 low down than high, and some everywhere. CO2 is heavier but it doesn't all fall to the bottom of a room. Brighter gas is denser gas.
