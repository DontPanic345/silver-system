The still

Juniper standing in water, in a pot held at 368 K, and a cold receiver on the other side of a wall with one gap in it. Gin comes out of the far end. Nothing here is a brewing mechanic: the pot is warm, so the botanicals ferment; the wash boils at 351.5 K and the water beside it boils at 373.15 K, so holding the pot between those two figures boils off one and not the other. That 22 kelvin gap is the whole of distilling.

The run

Step—
Gin in the receiver—
Mean temperature—

  1. Mash — juniper touching water above 310 K becomes wash. One row of the reaction table; nothing names juniper.
  2. Boil — wash past 351.5 K becomes spirit vapour, by the same phase change that boils a kettle.
  3. Cross — spirit vapour is denser than air (46 g/mol against 29), so it lies on top of the wash and creeps sideways through the gap rather than rising.
  4. Condense — on the cold floor it becomes gin, and pools.
  5. Drink — the two gnomes started thirsty.

Boiling off a gram of spirit costs 846 J and gives it back on condensing, so a still is a heat pump with a hole in it. The hob and the cold receiver are declared boundaries — every joule they move goes on the ledger, which is why the residuals above stay at floating-point noise while the still runs.
