Gnome terrarium

A warm spring under a pool, a glass lid held cold, a garden on a watered bed, and four gnomes on the meadow between them. Nothing below is scripted and nothing boils: the pool evaporates because warm water does, the humid air meets the cold lid, dew gathers there and falls back as drops. The jar also has a day — sunlight comes through the lid, warms what it lands on, and goes out again at dusk, and the whole scene dims with it. That day is what the garden runs on. In the light the bushes take carbon dioxide out of the air and water out of their bed and put on weight; in the dark they spend some of it back. The gnomes eat the bushes and breathe the carbon out again, so the jar's carbon goes round rather than accumulating — which you can watch below as carbon dioxide rising while oxygen falls. A magic fountain (an ethereal pipe, paid for in Gin) lifts water from one end of the pool to the other. The numbers on the right are the same JSON a headless run prints.

dig   build   warm or chill

You do not touch this world. You write on a cell and a gnome walks over and does it — after it has breathed and eaten, because a colony that digs while it suffocates is the thing this project exists to object to. Dig takes a cell of solid out of the world and puts it in a gnome's hands; build puts whatever it is carrying back down. There is no resource counter and no material palette: you cannot build what you have not dug, and the wall you get is the same grams at the same temperature as the hole you made. Watch "Carried in hand" against "Mass conjured" — they are the same number, because what is in a gnome's hands has left the world and the ledger is holding it. Warm and chill are the magic, and they cost Gin.

Controls

Cells are tinted warm when hotter than room temperature and cool when colder, and dimmed by how much daylight reaches them — so the whole jar darkens at night, and the ground under a bush or a shelf stays darker than the ground beside it. Pale dots are gnomes; a violet dot is a gnome in the ethereal layer, waiting for a rescue; a gnome with a coloured pip on its head is carrying that material.
